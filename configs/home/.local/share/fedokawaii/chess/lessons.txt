# the lessons of kawa-chess, in the order of the year: lesson.py says how they are read, tools/check-chess-lessons.py holds them to the board
=== the-board
title: the board
minutes: 3
source: Steps Method 1, lesson 1; chess.com Learn to Play

@fen 8/8/8/8/8/8/8/K6k w - - 0 1
@mark e4
sixty-four squares. the files run up the board, a to h; the ranks run across, 1 to 8. a square is named by its file, then its rank: this one is e4.

white sits at the bottom. the corner at your right hand is always a light square.
---
@find e4 a1 h8 d5 g2 b7
find each square as it is named: type its name, or click it. you will use this on every move from now on.
---
a move is typed (Rd7), or clicked, piece then square, or made with the arrows and enter. type when you can: the notation is how chess is read and written, and typing it teaches it.

one thing said once. no one on record has gone from nothing to 1800 in a single year. this year is built to try it, and your numbers will say plainly where you stand.

=== the-rook
title: the rook
minutes: 3
source: Steps Method 1; Seirawan, Play Winning Chess

@fen 8/8/8/8/3R4/8/8/K6k w - - 0 1
@mark d1 d2 d3 d5 d6 d7 d8 a4 b4 c4 e4 f4 g4 h4
the rook moves in straight lines, along its rank or its file, as far as the way is clear. it never jumps.
---
@fen 8/3p4/8/8/3R1P2/8/8/K6k w - - 0 1
@try Rxd7 | look up the d-file. type Rxd7, or click the rook and then the pawn.
it takes what stands in its way by landing on its square. your own pawn on f4 blocks it: it cannot pass. take the black pawn.
---
@fen 4k3/1p6/8/8/8/8/8/R3K3 w - - 0 1
@try Ra7 | stand on the pawn's rank with a clear way to it.
a rook attacks along its lines. attack the pawn on b7 from the side.

@mark a7 b6 c5 e3 f2 g1 c3 b2 a1 e5 f6 g7 h8

@fen 8/8/8/8/3Q4/8/8/K6k w - - 0 1

=== the-king
title: the king
minutes: 2
source: Steps Method 1; Seirawan, Play Winning Chess

@fen 8/8/8/8/3K4/8/8/7k w - - 0 1
@mark c3 d3 e3 c4 e4 c5 d5 e5
the king moves one square, any way. he is not strong, but the whole game is about him: trap him and you have won.
---
@fen 8/8/8/3k4/8/3K4/8/8 w - - 0 1

=== the-knight

@fen 8/8/8/8/3N4/8/8/K6k w - - 0 1
@mark b3 b5 c2 c6 e2 e6 f3 f5
the knight jumps: two squares in a line, then one to the side, an L. he lands on these squares and touches nothing between. nothing blocks him. he always lands on the other colour.
---
@fen 4k3/8/2p5/8/3N4/8/8/4K3 w - - 0 1
@try Nxc6 | two up, one to the left.
take the pawn on c6.
---
@fen 7k/8/4p3/8/8/8/8/N3K3 w - - 0 1
@try Nb3 Nc2 | from the corner he has two squares only.
a knight in the corner reaches little. bring him out.
---
@fen 7k/8/8/8/3p4/8/2N5/4K3 w - - 0 1
@try Nxd4 | two up, one to the right.
a knight's moves are the hardest to see at first, so look for them on purpose. take the pawn.

=== the-pawn
title: the pawn
minutes: 4
source: Steps Method 1, the pawn; Seirawan, Play Winning Chess

@fen 4k3/pppppppp/8/8/8/8/PPPPPPPP/4K3 w - - 0 1
@mark e3 e4
the pawn moves straight ahead, one square; from its first square it may go two. it never goes back, and anything in front of it blocks it.
---
@fen 4k3/8/8/3p4/4P3/8/8/4K3 w - - 0 1
@try exd5 | a pawn takes one square diagonally forward: exd5.
the pawn does not take straight ahead: it takes diagonally. take the black pawn.
---
@fen 4k3/8/8/4p3/4P3/8/8/4K3 w - - 0 1
two pawns face to face block each other: neither can move or take.
---
@fen 8/4P3/8/8/8/8/k7/4K3 w - - 0 1
@try e8=Q+ | move it to the last rank: type e8=Q.
a pawn that reaches the last rank becomes a queen, a rook, a bishop or a knight: your choice, nearly always the queen.

=== check
title: check
minutes: 3
source: Steps Method 1, check; lichess Learn: out of check

@fen 4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8/4RK2 b - - 0 1
the white rook attacks the black king along the e-file: that is check. a king may never stay attacked and may never step into attack: check must be answered at once.
---
@fen 4k3/4p3/8/8/8/8/4qP2/4K3 w - - 0 1
@try Kxe2 | nothing guards the queen.
there are three answers. first: take what gives the check.
---
@fen 6k1/8/8/8/8/5n2/8/4K3 w - - 0 1
@try Ke2 Kf2 Kf1 Kd1 | d2 is covered by the knight as well. step somewhere else.
second: move the king away. a knight's check can never be blocked: he jumps.
---
@fen 7k/8/8/8/2B5/8/8/4K2r w - - 0 1
@try Bf1 | the bishop can stand between the rook and the king.
third: put something between. your king guards f1, so the rook cannot take without losing itself.

=== checkmate
title: checkmate
minutes: 4
source: Steps Method 1, the mate lessons; Silman, Complete Endgame Course, part one

@fen 6k1/5ppp/8/8/8/8/5PPP/3R2K1 w - - 0 1
@try Rd8# | the rook goes to the last rank.
when the king is in check and none of the three answers exists, it is checkmate and the game is over. the black king is hemmed in by his own pawns. mate him.
---
@fen 7k/6pp/8/8/8/8/8/K5Q1 w - - 0 1
Qxg7 here would be check, but the king simply takes the queen. a check is nothing by itself: every answer has to be closed.
---
@fen 7k/6pp/8/8/8/8/1B6/K5Q1 w - - 0 1
@try Qxg7# | on g7 the bishop guards her.
with a bishop on the long diagonal the queen on g7 is guarded. now it is mate.
---
@fen 4k3/8/4K3/8/8/8/8/7R w - - 0 1
@try Rh8# | check along the last rank.
your king takes the squares in front of his; the rook gives the check. two pieces, working together.

=== stalemate
title: stalemate
minutes: 3
source: Silman, Complete Endgame Course, part one (stalemate awareness); Steps Method 1, the draw

@fen 7k/5Q2/6K1/8/8/8/8/8 b - - 0 1
black to move. he is not in check, and he has no legal move at all. that is stalemate: the game is a draw, and white, with everything, gets half a point.
---
@fen 7k/8/6K1/8/8/8/5Q2/8 w - - 0 1
@try Qf8# | Qf7 would leave him no move and no check: stalemate. a queen check on the last rank is the mate.
your move. look for the mate, and check that you are not about to do what you just saw.

=== castling
title: castling
minutes: 3
source: Steps Method 1, castling; chess.com Learn to Play

@fen r3k2r/pppppppp/8/8/8/8/PPPPPPPP/R3K2R w KQkq - 0 1
@try O-O O-O-O | type O-O, or click the king and then g1.
once a game, the king moves two squares towards a rook, and the rook jumps over to his other side. it hides the king and brings the rook out, in one move. O-O is the short side, O-O-O the long. castle.
---
not allowed: if the king or that rook has moved; if anything stands between them; if the king is in check; if he would cross or land on an attacked square.
---
@fen r3k2r/pppppppp/b7/8/8/8/PPPP1PPP/R3K2R w KQkq - 0 1
@try O-O-O | the bishop on a6 looks at f1: he may not cross it. the other side is free.
the short way is closed to you here. castle the other way.

=== promotion

minutes: 3
source: Steps Method 1; Silman, Complete Endgame Course, part one

@fen 8/P7/8/8/8/8/k7/4K3 w - - 0 1
@try a8=Q | a8, then the queen: type a8=Q.
a pawn on the last rank must become a queen, rook, bishop or knight. you may have two queens or more. every pawn is a queen waiting: that is why endings are fought over pawns.

=== notation-and-draws
title: notation and draws
minutes: 4
source: FIDE Laws of Chess, Appendix C; Steps Method 1, notation and the draw

@fen r1bqkbnr/pppp1ppp/2n5/4p3/4P3/5N2/PPPP1PPP/RNBQKB1R w KQkq - 2 3
@try Bb5 Bc4 | a bishop to b5 is Bb5, to c4 is Bc4.
K Q R B N name the pieces; a pawn has no letter. Nf3: a knight to f3. x takes: Bxc6. + is check, # is mate. if two knights can reach a square, say which: Nbd2. type a bishop move.
---
@fen 7k/8/8/8/8/8/8/K7 w - - 0 1

=== what-they-are-worth
title: what the pieces are worth
minutes: 3
source: Seirawan, Play Winning Chess (force); Steps Method 1, favourable exchange

@fen rnbqkbnr/pppppppp/8/8/8/8/PPPPPPPP/RNBQKBNR w KQkq - 0 1
pawn 1, knight 3, bishop 3, rook 5, queen 9. the king has no price: he is never taken. at your level most games are decided by who has more of these.

add it up before any trade. a rook for a knight: you give 5, you get 3. a queen for a rook: you give 9, you get 5.

to attack is to threaten to take. a pawn attacking a knight wins time: he must move. attack him.
---
@fen 4k3/8/8/3n4/2P5/8/8/4K3 b - - 0 1

@try Nb4 Nb6 Nc3 Nc7 Ne3 Ne7 Nf4 Nf6 | a knight move saves him.

=== is-it-safe
title: is it safe?
minutes: 4
source: Heisman, "Is it safe?" and Everyone's 2nd Chess Book; Steps Method's three questions

@fen r1bqkbnr/ppp2ppp/2np4/4p3/2B1P3/5N2/PPPP1PPP/RNBQK2R w KQkq - 0 4
@try O-O d3 c3 Nc3 | Bxf7+ Kxf7: your bishop for a pawn. something quieter.
ask of every move you are about to play: if I play this, can he reply with a check, a capture or a threat that I cannot meet? assume yes, and try to prove yourself wrong. Bxf7+ looks fierce. play something safe instead.
---
@fen rnb1kbnr/pppp1ppp/5q2/4p3/4P3/5N2/PPPP1PPP/RNBQKB1R w KQkq - 2 3
then, when he has moved, ask: what does that move do? what does it attack, what does it stop guarding? his queen came to f6 and looks at your knight. count: your queen and your g-pawn guard it. no threat. carry on.
---
this is the difference between hope and chess. the games this week ask only that: not one piece given away.

=== loose-pieces
title: loose pieces
minutes: 3
source: Heisman, Hope Chess vs Real Chess (seeds of tactical destruction); Lichess theme hangingPiece

@fen r1b1k2r/ppppqppp/2n2n2/2b1p3/2B1P3/2N2N2/PPPPQPPP/R1B1K2R w KQkq - 6 6
