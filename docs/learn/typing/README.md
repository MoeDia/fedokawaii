# Typing

Touch typing, from the first two keys to 100 words a minute and past it. Super+U → learning → typing, or Typing in the
launcher. Twenty minutes a day; the first row always knows what comes next.

## What you see

One stage on every screen: the keyboard (your own: the K617, 61 keys) and two hands stay where they are, the demon's in the
dark, the angel's in the light; what the screen is about stands over them: the list, the text you type, a result. The next key
is lit, and the finger that goes to it wears the same colour: little pink, ring yellow, middle green, index blue, on both
hands; the thumbs gold, for the space bar. A capital lights two keys: its own, and the shift of the other hand. Keys held on
the real keyboard go down on the screen. A wrong key flashes red. A run with no slip lifts embers from the demon's hands,
petals from the angel's.

## Keys

| key | does |
|---|---|
| Enter | the next thing, on every screen |
| Esc | out of a lesson (it is kept); out of a practice run (it is not); back; close |
| Backspace | takes a letter back (from the sentences on) |
| Tab | answers the eye |
| Ctrl+K | quiets the next key's light (the keyboard stays), or brings it back |
| r | a result: the same lesson again, on fresh text |
| Tab, ← → | on the tabs: course, practice, progress |

`kawa-typing FILE` types any file of yours: code, notes, whatever you want your hands to know.

## How it teaches

- **Keys come two at a time**, one from each hand, in EdClub's order: the home row, then up, then down, then capitals,
  numbers, symbols. A key's first lesson has no score. Fingers go back home after every key; feel the bumps on f and j.
- **Accuracy first.** Under 97 % a lesson does not pass, however fast. At 97 % it passes, and the speed earns its seals.
  Five seals need the goal and 99 %; the gold one, half as fast again without one slip.
- **Look at the screen.** Now and then, in sentences and passages, an eye opens under the text. Press Tab within 2 s, or the
  text dims and waits. You only miss it by looking at your hands.
- **The first minute** measures you as you type today, whatever way. Touch typing feels slower than that for a long time, and
  it is where most people give up and go back to their old way. The progress page keeps that number until you pass it.
- **Twenty minutes, daily.** Hour for hour, short and often beats long and rare: the most famous study of this, on postmen
  learning to type, found that an hour a day learned more from each hour than four hours a day (which still finished sooner by
  the calendar), and the ones trained best enjoyed it least. It says so when you have done enough.
- **Then speed**: passages from Okakura, Hearn and Nitobe, the commonest letter patterns, real code, and a pacer: a second caret
  running at the goal. From 30 wpm to 110, five at a time, always at 97 %.

## What the numbers mean

- **wpm**: five characters are a word; only what stands correct counts, the clock from the first key to the last. Pauses of
  more than 5 s and time out of the window do not count.
- **accuracy**: right presses ÷ all presses. An error corrected is still an error.
- **consistency**: how even your speed was, second by second (Monkeytype's measure).
- **rollover**: how often a key goes down before the last one comes up. The fast typists of a study of 168,000 people did it on
  half of their keys, the slow ones almost never. Nothing to practise: it comes with speed.
- **The progress page**: your tests against the marks (the average typist 52, the fast ones 90, your goal 100), every day you
  practised, each key by how sure you are of it; and, after two weeks, when your own line reaches 100.
